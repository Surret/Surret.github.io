The Rapid Racking Hook

A custom-designed and 3D-printed hook to hold a set of ear defenders on a rapid racking system when not in use. This project focused on creating a practical and robust component that fit an existing industrial design.

This project was a response to a specific need for a practical solution in an industrial setting. The goal was to create a custom hook that would seamlessly integrate with an existing rapid racking system, providing a dedicated place for ear defenders. The design needed to be functional, reliable, and strong enough for its intended purpose.

I ended up designing and printing six hooks in total to hold various items, demonstrating the adaptability and utility of a well-designed component.

Key Challenges & Solutions

  • Precise Measurement & Fitting: The racking system used a "keyhole" design, which required me to take precise measurements of the holes. The hook's pegs had to fit snugly without being too tight, requiring a small gap between the size of the hole and the pegs.
  • Designing for Strength: To ensure the hook could hold the weight of the ear defenders, I designed it with two well-spaced pegs. This distribution of weight made the hook strong enough for its intended purpose and was even able to hold a light jacket without issue.

Personal Learning

  • Designing for a Specific Use Case: This project taught me the importance of designing for a specific, real-world need rather than just for maximum strength. The final product was perfectly suited for holding ear defenders, which was the core requirement.
  • Working with Existing Structures: I gained valuable experience in designing a component that had to integrate with an existing physical structure. This requires careful measurement and a deep understanding of how a new part will interact with a pre-existing one.

Tools & Technologies

  • 3D Modelling: Fusion 360
  • 3D Printer: Creality Ender 3 V2
  • Slicing Software: Cura
  • Materials: PLA Filament (Black)

Future Possibilities

  • Exploring Other Materials: While PLA was suitable for this project, a future version could be printed in a stronger material like PETG or ABS for a heavier load or more rugged environment.
